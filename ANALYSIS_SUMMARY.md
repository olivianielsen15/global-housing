# Global Housing Data Analysis: Key Insights Across 91 Countries & 19 Metrics

## Executive Summary

Analysis of global housing data reveals powerful correlations, surprising outliers, and clear evidence of what works—and what doesn't—in housing policy worldwide. The data shows that **policy actually matters**, that **building code age is destiny**, and that **material costs are real barriers**, not just excuses.

## 1. STRONG CORRELATIONS: The Iron Laws of Housing

### The "Death Spiral" Correlation (r > 0.93)
**Housing deficit, informal housing, cost burden, and market mismatch move together in lockstep:**

- **Housing Deficit ↔ Informal Housing: r = 0.959**
  - Where housing shortage exists, informal settlements flourish
  - Ethiopia: 38.6/1000 deficit, 71.4% informal
  - Madagascar: 37.6/1000 deficit, 70.3% informal
  - Compare to Austria: 2.2/1000 deficit, 0.6% informal

- **Housing Deficit ↔ Housing Mismatch: r = 0.951**
  - Countries building the wrong housing have the worst deficits
  - Angola: 33.6/1000 deficit, 91.4 mismatch score
  - Finland: 2.1/1000 deficit, 8.8 mismatch score

- **Cost Burden ↔ Housing Mismatch: r = 0.941**
  - When markets build luxury over affordability, people suffer
  - Sudan: 43.6% burdened, 96.0 mismatch
  - Austria: 7.1% burdened, 11.8 mismatch

### The Policy Effectiveness Correlation (r = -0.863)
**Policy Achievement ↔ Deficit Improvement: Strong NEGATIVE correlation**

Countries with high policy achievement see rapid deficit reduction:
- **Austria**: Policy 56.2 → Deficit improving -8.4/1000 by 2034
- **Singapore**: Policy 55.6 → Deficit improving -7.8/1000 by 2034
- **Denmark**: Policy 54.4 → Deficit improving -8.0/1000 by 2034
- **France**: Policy 54.5 → Deficit improving -7.6/1000 by 2034

**The evidence is clear: Policy works when properly funded and executed.**

### The Building Code Correlation (r = 0.859)
**Deficit Projection 2034 ↔ Building Code Age: Strong POSITIVE correlation**

**Ancient codes = Ancient problems:**
- Countries with 100-year-old codes: Average 60.6% informal housing
- Countries with modern codes (≤3 years): Average 9.4% informal housing

**Top modern code performers:**
- Singapore: 0yr code, 0.3% informal
- Netherlands: 1yr code, 0.6% informal
- United States: 0yr code, 2.4% informal

**Ancient code laggards:**
- Sudan: 100yr code, 73.8% informal
- Ethiopia: 100yr code, 71.4% informal
- Tanzania: 100yr code, 62.3% informal

### The Construction Momentum Correlation (r = -0.849)
**Construction Jobs ↔ Deficit Improvement: Strong NEGATIVE correlation**

Building more = Better outcomes:
- **UAE**: 156.3 construction jobs/1000, improving -15.5/1000
- **Singapore**: 121.5 jobs/1000, improving -7.8/1000
- **China**: 97.4 jobs/1000, improving -6.8/1000

Low construction = Worsening deficits:
- **Madagascar**: 18.2 jobs/1000, 37.6/1000 deficit
- **Mozambique**: 19.2 jobs/1000, 37.1/1000 deficit

### The Affordability Paradox (r = -0.813)
**Affordable Home Price Ratio ↔ Housing Mismatch: Strong NEGATIVE**

When mortgage capacity is high (low rates), markets build FOR people, not AGAINST them.

- **Worst performers**:
  - Sudan: 100yr code, 73.8% informal
  - Ethiopia: 100yr code, 71.4% informal
  - Madagascar: 100yr code, 70.3% informal
  - Mozambique: 100yr code, 69.8% informal
  - Uganda: 100yr code, 68.9% informal

### The Building Code Gradient

**Correlation: Code Age ↔ Informal Housing = 0.830**

Every decade of code age adds ~6 percentage points to informal housing share.

**Code Age Bands:**
- **0-3 years**: 9.4% informal (modern standards)
- **4-10 years**: ~15% informal (moderate lag)
- **11-20 years**: ~25% informal (significant obsolescence)
- **50+ years**: 60.6% informal (colonial-era codes, catastrophic)

### Why Code Age Matters

1. **Safety standards**: Seismic, fire, flood protection
2. **Efficiency standards**: Energy, water, waste
3. **Accessibility**: Disability access, universal design
4. **Density allowances**: Modern codes enable multi-family, mixed-use
5. **Process efficiency**: Digital permitting, streamlined approvals

**Old codes = Old priorities**: Many century-old codes prioritize aesthetics (colonial architecture), single-family only, low density, manual approvals. They're barriers to modern, efficient, affordable housing.

## 10. KEY TAKEAWAYS

### What The Data Proves:

1. **Policy works** (r = -0.863 with deficit improvement)
2. **Building codes matter** (r = 0.830 with informal housing)
3. **Construction momentum matters** (r = -0.849 with deficit improvement)
4. **Material costs are real barriers**, not excuses (landlocked Africa)
5. **Social housing reduces cost burden** (correlation clear)
6. **Land affordability enables supply** (Iceland, Austria vs Angola, Ethiopia)
7. **Green vs Resilience is a strategic choice** based on disaster risk
8. **High spending works** (Denmark, Netherlands) **and low spending works** (USA, Singapore)—the strategy matters more than the budget

### The Uncomfortable Truth:

**Housing crises are POLICY CHOICES, not inevitabilities.**

Countries with similar GDP per capita have wildly different outcomes:
- **Austria vs Luxembourg**: Same wealth, Austria has 11.8 mismatch vs Luxembourg's 41.3
- **Singapore vs Hong Kong-level density**: Singapore has 0.8/1000 deficit through construction
- **Nordic countries vs UK**: All wealthy, but social housing % determines burden levels

### The Path Forward:

**For Developed Countries:**
- Update building codes (UK: 11yr old, Ireland: 12yr old)
- Increase social housing (US: 1.8%, Australia: 4.6%)
- Reduce mismatch through affordable mandates
- Maintain construction momentum

**For Developing Countries:**
- Invest in cement production (ends import dependency)
- Reform land ownership/tenure (enables formal construction)
- Update colonial-era building codes (16 countries at 100+ years)
- Focus on resilience certification in disaster-prone areas

**For Landlocked African Countries:**
- Regional cement factories (cooperative investment)
- Cross-border infrastructure (reduce transport costs)
- Land reform (break 15-99 month pricing)
- Modern codes + enforcement (end informal trap)
